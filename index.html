⛩️ PILLAR I

WHAT IS TOWER OF HANOI?

起源と伝説 • THE COSMIC TEMPLE OF BENARES

The Tower of Hanoi (also known as Lucas' Tower or the Tower of Brahma) is one of the most famous mathematical puzzles ever devised. It is a profound meditation on order, exponential growth, and algorithmic recursion.

💎 The 64 Golden Needles of Lord Brahma

According to ancient Indian myth, beneath the golden dome of the temple of Kashi Vishwanath in holy Benares (Varanasi), there rests a brass plinth mounted with three diamond needles, each cubits high and slender as a wasp.

At the dawn of the universe, Lord Brahma placed 64 disks of pure beaten gold upon the first needle—the largest disk resting on the brass base, and each subsequent disk smaller than the one beneath it.

A brotherhood of ascetic priests was sworn to transfer all 64 disks from the first needle to the third needle, moving one disk per day without ever violating the sacred laws of Brahma. The prophecy warns: when the final golden disk is set upon the third needle, the temple will crumble into fine ash, and the entire universe will conclude its cycle!

⚡ PILLAR III

THE ART OF RECURSION & 2N − 1

再帰の極意 • DIVIDE, CONQUER AND EXPONENTIAL GROWTH

How does a samurai conquer an intimidating mountain of N disks without guessing? By mastering Recursion—the mathematical discipline of solving a grand challenge by breaking it into identical smaller sub-battles!

KATA 1
Clear the Mountain Path
T(N − 1) moves

To free the largest base disk N, you must first recursively move the top (N − 1) disks from Tower A (Origin) to Tower B (Auxiliary), using Tower C as temporary rest.

KATA 2
The Shogun's Strike
Exactly 1 move

With the path completely cleared, move the single largest base disk Disk N directly from Tower A (Origin) to Tower C (Target)!

KATA 3
Reunite the Tower
T(N − 1) moves

Now recursively move the (N − 1) disks from Tower B (Auxiliary) onto Tower C (Target) right on top of disk N. Victory is achieved!

⚔️ MATHEMATICAL PROOF OF 2N − 1

Summing the 3 katas reveals the recurrence formula for minimum moves T(N):

T(N) = T(N − 1) + 1 + T(N − 1) = 2 × T(N − 1) + 1
N = 1 T(1) = 1 = 21 − 1 1 move
N = 2 T(2) = 2(1) + 1 = 3 = 22 − 1 3 moves
N = 3 T(3) = 2(3) + 1 = 7 = 23 − 1 7 moves
N = 4 T(4) = 2(7) + 1 = 15 = 24 − 1 15 moves

Every single disk added doubles the moves plus one. That is why recursion in Hanoi grows exponentially (O(2N))!

📜 THE SAMURAI GUIDE TO RECURSION

1. The Core Principle of Recursion

Recursion is solving a problem by breaking it down into smaller instances of the exact same problem until reaching a simple base case.

Hanoi(N, Source, Target, Auxiliary):
  If N == 1:
    Move Disk 1 from Source → Target  // Base Case
  Else:
    Hanoi(N-1, Source, Auxiliary, Target)  // Step 1
    Move Disk N from Source → Target     // Step 2
    Hanoi(N-1, Auxiliary, Target, Source)  // Step 3

2. Mathematical Proof: $2^n - 1$

To move $n$ disks:

  • Move top $n-1$ disks out of the way: $T(n-1)$ moves
  • Move bottom disk $n$ to destination: 1 move
  • Move top $n-1$ disks onto disk $n$: $T(n-1)$ moves

$$T(n) = 2 \cdot T(n-1) + 1 = 2^n - 1$$
